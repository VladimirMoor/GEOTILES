# Theorem: Minkowski sums of simplices of total dimension ≤ 4 tile ℝ³

## Statement

**Setting.** Let Δ₁, …, Δ_k ⊂ ℝ³ be segments and triangles in general position, meaning that every 3×3 determinant formed from edge vectors of the summands is non-zero. Let P = Δ₁ ⊕ … ⊕ Δ_k. Suppose the total dimension Σ dim Δᵢ is 3 or 4.

**Theorem.** P tiles ℝ³ face-to-face by translates of P and of its point reflection −P.

| Σ dim | Partition | Body | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1+1+1 | parallelepiped | classical |
| 3 | 2+1 | triangular prism | classical: half a parallelepiped |
| 4 | 1+1+1+1 | zonotope with 4 generators | McMullen (1975): parallelohedron |
| 4 | 2+1+1 | parallelogram ⊕ triangle (10 faces) | **proved here** |
| 4 | 2+2 | triangle ⊕ triangle (8 or 9 faces) | **proved here** |

**Equilateral corollaries.** With unit triangles and unit segments we obtain:
- the generalized gyrobifastigium (8 faces);
- the 9-face body triangle ⊕ triangle (4 triangles + 5 rhombi);
- the 10-face body rhombus ⊕ triangle (2 triangles + 8 rhombi).

All three are equilateral convex space-fillers.

## Proof (computer-assisted, exact) — `prove_tiling.py`

1. **Affine normalization.** A tiling by translations and point reflections is mapped to a tiling of the same kind by any affine map. So it is enough to treat:
   - T₁ = {0, e₁, e₂} and T₂ = {0, e₃, v};
   - Par = {0, e₁, e₁+e₂, e₂} and T = {0, e₃, v};

   with v = (a, b, c). Up to affine equivalence, these are all pairs in general position.
2. **Chambers.** General position means the elementary determinants are non-zero. For triangle ⊕ triangle these are the linear forms a, b, a+b, c, c−1; for parallelogram ⊕ triangle they are a, b, c, c−1. The chambers are a sector in the (a, b)-plane times an interval in c: 18 and 12 chambers. On each chamber the combinatorics of P is fixed.
3. **Linearity.** In every determinant that occurs, the vector v appears at most once, so every supporting and separating form is affine-linear in (a, b, c). Its sign on a whole chamber is decided exactly at the vertices and rays of the chamber's closure.
4. **Poincaré conditions, checked in each chamber.**
   - (i) **Supporting planes.** Every face F of P is supporting: n_F·(x − x_F) is identically 0 on F and < 0 on the other vertices.
   - (ii) **Face pairings.** For every face F there is γ_F in the group generated by the lattice L and the point reflection, and a face G with γ_F(G) = F. These are symbolic identities. The image γ_F P lies strictly on the other side of F, by linear sign certificates.
   - (ii′) **Consistency.** The pairing of G is exactly γ_F⁻¹.
   - (iii) **Edge cycles.** Following the pairings around every edge returns to P with identity holonomy (a symbolic identity). The dihedral angles along the cycle sum to 2π at a sample point. This sum is continuous and a multiple of 2π, so it equals 2π on the whole chamber.
5. **Conclusion.** Apply Poincaré's polyhedron theorem in the Euclidean case: a compact convex polytope with isometric face pairings and the edge-cycle condition is a fundamental domain of the group generated by its pairings, so the images of P tile ℝ³.

**Result:** 12/12 chambers (parallelogram ⊕ triangle) and 18/18 chambers (triangle ⊕ triangle) proved. Running time is about 50 s.

**Lattices.** Write T₁ = o₁+{0,u₁,u₂} and T₂ = o₂+{0,v₁,v₂} with a suitable labelling.

| Body | Lattice | Point reflection x ↦ w − x |
|---|---|---|
| triangle ⊕ triangle, 8 faces | ⟨v₁, u₁, u₂−v₂⟩ | w = 2(o₁+o₂)+v₁+u₁+u₂ |
| triangle ⊕ triangle, 9 faces | ⟨u₁+v₁, u₁+v₂, u₂−u₁⟩ | w = 2(o₁+o₂) |
| parallelogram ⊕ triangle | ⟨r₁+r₂+t₁, r₁+r₂+t₂, r₁−r₂+t₁−t₂⟩ | w = 2(o+o_T)+r₁+t₁ |

## Open

- **Converse.** Do sums of total dimension ≥ 5 never tile? All 24 random members tested fail the dihedral filter, which proves non-tiling for those members, but there is no general proof.
- **Special positions.** These are the chamber walls, for example F7-029 and F8-249. Explicit tilings are known numerically; a wall-by-wall run of the same prover is still to be done.
- **Is every equilateral space-filler a Minkowski sum of unit polygons and segments?**
- **Higher dimensions.** Do sums of simplices with Σ dim = d+1 tile ℝ^d in general?
