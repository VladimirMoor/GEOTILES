# Theorem: Minkowski sums of simplices of total dimension ≤ 4 tile ℝ³

## Statement

**Setting.** Let Δ₁, …, Δ_k ⊂ ℝ³ be segments and triangles in general position, meaning that every 3×3 determinant formed from edge vectors of the summands is non-zero. Let P = Δ₁ ⊕ … ⊕ Δ_k. Suppose the total dimension Σ dim Δᵢ is 3 or 4.

**Theorem.** P tiles ℝ³ face-to-face by translates of P and of its point reflection −P.

| Σ dim | Partition | Body | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1+1+1 | parallelepiped | classical |
| 3 | 2+1 | triangular prism | classical: half a parallelepiped |
| 4 | 1+1+1+1 | zonotope with 4 generators | McMullen (1975): parallelohedron |
| 4 | 2+1+1 | parallelogram ⊕ triangle (10 faces) | **proved here** |
| 4 | 2+2 | triangle ⊕ triangle (8 or 9 faces) | **proved here** |

**Equilateral corollaries.** With unit triangles and unit segments we obtain:
- the generalized gyrobifastigium (8 faces);
- the 9-face body triangle ⊕ triangle (4 triangles + 5 rhombi);
- the 10-face body rhombus ⊕ triangle (2 triangles + 8 rhombi).

All three are equilateral convex space-fillers.

## Proof (computer-assisted, exact) — `prove_tiling.py`

1. **Affine normalization.** A tiling by translations and point reflections is mapped to a tiling of the same kind by any affine map. So it is enough to treat:
   - T₁ = {0, e₁, e₂} and T₂ = {0, e₃, v};
   - Par = {0, e₁, e₁+e₂, e₂} and T = {0, e₃, v};

   with v = (a, b, c). Up to affine equivalence, these are all pairs in general position.
2. **Chambers.** General position means the elementary determinants are non-zero. For triangle ⊕ triangle these are the linear forms a, b, a+b, c, c−1; for parallelogram ⊕ triangle they are a, b, c, c−1. The chambers are a sector in the (a, b)-plane times an interval in c: 18 and 12 chambers. On each chamber the combinatorics of P is fixed.
3. **Linearity.** In every determinant that occurs, the vector v appears at most once, so every supporting and separating form is affine-linear in (a, b, c). Its sign on a whole chamber is decided exactly at the vertices and rays of the chamber's closure.
4. **Poincaré conditions, checked in each chamber.**
   - (i) **Supporting planes.** Every face F of P is supporting: n_F·(x − x_F) is identically 0 on F and < 0 on the other vertices.
   - (ii) **Face pairings.** For every face F there is γ_F in the group generated by the lattice L and the point reflection, and a face G with γ_F(G) = F. These are symbolic identities. The image γ_F P lies strictly on the other side of F, by linear sign certificates.
   - (ii′) **Consistency.** The pairing of G is exactly γ_F⁻¹.
   - (iii) **Edge cycles.** Following the pairings around every edge returns to P with identity holonomy (a symbolic identity). The dihedral angles along the cycle sum to 2π at a sample point. This sum is continuous and a multiple of 2π, so it equals 2π on the whole chamber.
5. **Conclusion.** Apply Poincaré's polyhedron theorem in the Euclidean case: a compact convex polytope with isometric face pairings and the edge-cycle condition is a fundamental domain of the group generated by its pairings, so the images of P tile ℝ³.

**Result:** 12/12 chambers (parallelogram ⊕ triangle) and 18/18 chambers (triangle ⊕ triangle) proved. Running time is about 50 s.

**Lattices.** Write T₁ = o₁+{0,u₁,u₂} and T₂ = o₂+{0,v₁,v₂} with a suitable labelling.

| Body | Lattice | Point reflection x ↦ w − x |
|---|---|---|
| triangle ⊕ triangle, 8 faces | ⟨v₁, u₁, u₂−v₂⟩ | w = 2(o₁+o₂)+v₁+u₁+u₂ |
| triangle ⊕ triangle, 9 faces | ⟨u₁+v₁, u₁+v₂, u₂−u₁⟩ | w = 2(o₁+o₂) |
| parallelogram ⊕ triangle | ⟨r₁+r₂+t₁, r₁+r₂+t₂, r₁−r₂+t₁−t₂⟩ | w = 2(o+o_T)+r₁+t₁ |

## Special positions (chamber walls)

On the walls the tilings stop being face-to-face. For example, a triangle merges with a rhombus into a pentagon, and the neighbour meets only part of it. So Poincaré's theorem cannot be applied verbatim. On all 70 wall cells the chamber formulas still give a tiling numerically (`data/walls.log`: a formula is found and the coverage test passes everywhere).

A limit argument settles the walls rigorously.

**Lemma.** Let P_t → P₀ in the Hausdorff metric, with P₀ 3-dimensional. Let the lattices L_t → L₀ and the centres w_t → w₀, and suppose that for every t > 0 the images of P_t under ⟨L_t, x ↦ w_t − x⟩ tile ℝ³ (with the same integer data). Then the images of P₀ under ⟨L₀, x ↦ w₀ − x⟩ tile ℝ³.

*Proof.*
1. The relation det L_t = 2 vol P_t holds for t > 0 and passes to the limit, so det L₀ = 2 vol P₀ > 0 and L₀ is a lattice.
2. *Covering.* For x ∈ ℝ³ and small t, the element γ_t with x ∈ γ_t P_t has bounded integer data. Take a subsequence on which the data are constant; since the tiles are closed, x ∈ γ₀ P₀.
3. *Disjointness.* If two limit tiles overlapped in an interior point, they would contain a common small ball. Then for small t the corresponding tiles γ_t P_t and γ′_t P_t would also overlap in interior points. These are distinct tiles of a tiling, which is a contradiction. ∎

In each chamber the prover verified **one fixed formula on the whole chamber**, so the lemma extends it to the closure of the chamber. P degenerates only when the triangle T₂ collapses (a = b = 0). This gives the corollary.

**Corollary.** For **any** two triangles T₁, T₂, and for any parallelogram and triangle, whose Minkowski sum is 3-dimensional, the sum tiles ℝ³ by translations and point reflections. Special positions are included: the gyrobifastigium itself, F7-029, F8-249, and the pentagonal prisms on the walls a = 0 and b = 0.

## The converse: total dimension ≥ 5 (2026-10-08)

**Claim.** For segments and triangles in general position with Σ dim ≥ 5, almost every sum does not tile ℝ³.

The claim "no member ever tiles" is probably false in this strict form: special members can satisfy angle relations by accident.

**Method** (`converse.py`). Fix a member p₀ and an edge class e. Find all non-negative integer relations Σ kᵢθᵢ(p₀) ∈ {π, 2π} with kₑ ≥ 1. The search is complete, because Σk ≤ 2π/θ_min. Keep those that are identities on the chamber, meaning the gradient Σ kᵢ∇θᵢ also vanishes; this is computed in 60-digit arithmetic.

If no relation exists at p₀ at all, call e a *witness*. Then:
1. p₀ fails the dihedral filter, so it does not tile.
2. The failure is an open condition, so the same holds on a neighbourhood of p₀.
3. Every relation that would let e pass is a non-trivial analytic equation, so the members of the chamber that pass the filter form a set of measure zero.

**Results.** 300 random members, about 240 distinct combinatorial types (chambers).

| Σ dim | Types | Witnesses per member | Members without a witness |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | tri⊕tri, tri⊕seg⊕seg, 4 segments | 0 (every edge covered by identities) | 90 / 90 |
| 5 | tri⊕tri⊕seg, tri⊕3 segs, 5 segs | 14–22 | **0 / 90** |
| 6 | tri⊕tri⊕tri, tri⊕tri⊕2 segs, tri⊕4 segs, 6 segs | 23–47 | **0 / 120** |

**Rigorous examples** (`converse_certify.py`). We used rational (Cayley) rotations and interval arithmetic for √3 and all angles. **23 of 25** sampled sums with Σ dim ∈ {5, 6} are *proved* not to tile, together with an open neighbourhood of each. The other 2 samples are 5-segment sums whose rational rotations happened to make generators parallel (12 faces instead of 20). Such a sum is a 4-generator zonotope and does tile, which shows that the general-position assumption is essential.

**Structure behind it.** Numerically, the only identities among the dihedral angles are the zone identities Σ_{zone} θ = (m − 1)π, where m is the number of edges of the zone. Zones with m ≥ 4 edges contribute no filter relation (π or 2π), so their edges become witnesses.

**Special positions can tile.** The generalized elongated gyrobifastigium (F8-255) is tri ⊕ tri ⊕ seg with both triangle planes containing the segment, and it tiles.

**Still open.** A proof for *every* chamber (we sampled about 240), and a proof that the zone identities are the only identities.
