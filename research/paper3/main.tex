\title{Dirichlet stereohedra: certified lower bounds and local rigidity}
\author{Vladimir Muravev}
\date{October 10, 2026}

\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
The largest known number of facets of a convex polyhedron that tiles $\R^3$ by congruent copies is $38$, attained by
Engel's Dirichlet stereohedra for the space group $I4_132$ (1981). We make four contributions.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item We point out that the statement ``$38$ is the maximum for plesiohedra'', which appears in standard references,
is not proved in the cited source. That source is a numerical grid search.
\item We search all space groups with numbers $75$--$230$ again, including lattice metrics outside the previously
sampled range, and certify every facet count in exact rational arithmetic. We find no Dirichlet stereohedron with
more than $38$ facets.
\item For ten space groups we obtain new certified lower bounds for the maximal number of facets of their Dirichlet
stereohedra. For example, $I4_1/a$ has a Dirichlet stereohedron with $29$ facets; the best previously reported value
was $23$.
\item We prove, by an exact computation, that Engel's $38$-facet tile is locally rigid. Near it there is no isohedral
tile with the same combinatorics that is not a Dirichlet cell. Single non-regular flips of the dual complex admit no
convex realization nearby.
\end{enumerate}
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem and its status}
Problem~(G) of Gr\"unbaum and Shephard~\cite{GS80} asks for the least upper bound on the number of facets of a convex
polytope that tiles $\R^n$ by congruent copies. For $n\ge 3$ it is open, and it is not even known whether such a bound
exists.

For a stereohedron, that is, the tile of an isohedral tiling, Delone's theorem bounds the number of facets by
$2^n(a+1)-2$, where $a$ is the number of aspects. In $\R^3$ this gives $390$, and Tarasov improved it to $378$. A
\emph{Dirichlet (Voronoi) stereohedron} is the Voronoi cell of one point $x$ in its orbit $\Gamma x$ under a space group
$\Gamma$. For these, Bochi\c{s}, Santos and Sabariego~\cite{BS01,BS06,SS08,SS11} proved bounds group by group. The
largest of their bounds is $92$, for the quarter cubic groups.

The record of $38$ facets is due to Engel~\cite{Eng81}. Schmitt~\cite{Sch16} computed the Dirichlet stereohedra of all
$145$ tetragonal, trigonal, hexagonal and cubic space groups on fine point grids. He confirmed Engel's four
combinatorial types with $f$-vector $(70,106,38)$ and found nothing larger. Wikipedia and MathWorld cite~\cite{Sch16}
for the claim that $38$ is the maximum for plesiohedra. However, \S2.3 of~\cite{Sch16} describes a grid search, and its
\S2.1 states problem~(G) as open. As far as we can tell, \textbf{the maximality of $38$ is not proved}.

Two features of the grid search matter below.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The $38$-facet chamber of $I4_132$ is covered by only $153$ of about $10^9$ grid points, so chambers smaller
than a grid cell could be missed.
\item For non-cubic groups the ratio $c/a$ was sampled only in $[\tfrac12,\tfrac72]$, with about $10^6$ position
points for each value.
\end{itemize}

\section{Exact certificates}
We work in fractional coordinates with the metric tensor $M=B^{\mathsf T}B$. This tensor is rational whenever $(c/a)^2$
is rational, also for hexagonal lattices. The Voronoi cell of a rational point $x$ is
\[
D(x)=\{z:\ 2(y-x)^{\mathsf T}Mz\le y^{\mathsf T}My-x^{\mathsf T}Mx\ \ \text{for all } y\in\Gamma x\setminus\{x\}\}.
\]
A \emph{certificate} that $D(x)$ has at least $k$ facets consists of $k$ distinct bisector planes, each containing three
non-collinear points of $D(x)$ that are verified exactly to be feasible. The points are vertices found in floating
point and then recomputed exactly as intersections of three planes. It suffices to use the orbit points $y$ with
$|y-x|<2\rho_L$, where $\rho_L$ is the covering radius of the lattice. Indeed, $D(x)$ is contained in the Voronoi cell of
the lattice $x+L\subset\Gamma x$.

The method reproduces Engel's $(70,106,38)$ at Schmitt's point $(427,761,1421)/6984$, and Schmitt's value $35$ for
$I4_122$ at $c/a=727/500$, both exactly.

\section{Search and new lower bounds}
For each of the $156$ groups with numbers $75$--$230$ we proceeded in three stages.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item We sampled $8\,000$ random points, with $c/a\in[0.3,4]$ for non-cubic groups.
\item We ran $25$ local hill-climbs, which maximise the facet count and, as a tie-breaker, minimise the gap to the
nearest non-facet bisector. This steers the search into small chambers.
\item We certified the best points exactly.
\end{enumerate}
For the $71$ groups without mirror planes we also searched $c/a\in[0.05,0.5]$ and $c/a\in[3.5,20]$.

Floating-point runs reported up to $58$ facets. These were artefacts: points within about $10^{-12}$ of a mirror plane,
where nearly coincident planes were counted twice. The exact certificates at the same places give Schmitt's values. We
report only certified numbers.

\section{Local rigidity of Dirichlet stereohedra}
Fix a tile $P$ of an isohedral face-to-face tiling with group $\Gamma$, and fix its combinatorial type. The tiling
conditions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{(L)} for each neighbour $\gamma$, the map $\gamma$ takes the vertices of the facet $F_{\gamma^{-1}}$ to those of
$F_\gamma$; these equations are linear in the vertices;
\item \textbf{(P)} every facet is planar.
\end{itemize}
Every solution near a tiling is again a tiling. Neighbouring copies meet along facets, and the angle sums around edges
are continuous multiples of $2\pi$, so they remain equal to $2\pi$. Both conditions are affine-invariant and rational in
fractional coordinates. We compute the rank of the Jacobian of (L)+(P) exactly, modulo two $31$-bit primes, at exact
vertex coordinates. We also compute exactly the tangent directions of the Dirichlet family: moving $x$, and changing
$c/a$ for non-cubic groups.

\begin{theorem}[computer-assisted]
For Engel's $38$-facet tile ($I4_132$, $x=(427,761,1421)/6984$) the solution set of (L)+(P) has tangent space of
dimension exactly $3$, spanned by the motions of $x$. Hence, to first order, every isohedral face-to-face tile near it
with the same combinatorics is a Dirichlet stereohedron. The same holds for Schmitt's $35$-facet tile of $I4_122$, with
dimension $4$ (motions of $x$ and the change of $c/a$), and for $46$ further generic Dirichlet stereohedra in $13$ groups
with $12$ to $38$ facets.
\end{theorem}

Floating-point versions of this computation suggested one or two extra directions. That was an artefact of edges of
length about $10^{-9}$.

We also built the $\Gamma$-invariant dual complex of Engel's tiling from the exact cell. A dual cell must contain all
orbit points equidistant from the vertex, not only the facet neighbours; the dual cells contain $4$ or $6$ sites. We then
tried all $26$ equivariant $2\to3$ flips that give the cell a new neighbour. None admits a nearby realization satisfying
(L)+(P) with the new facet opened, even to area $10^{-6}$. The residual stays between $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-2}$, and the
resulting tiles are not convex. Non-Dirichlet stereohedra with more facets, if they exist, are therefore not obtained by
local modification of the Dirichlet records.
